## Classical Bits vs. Qubits

A classical bit is always **0** or **1**. A qubit can exist in a *superposition* of both states at once:

$$|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$$

where $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$. Measuring the qubit collapses it to $|0\rangle$ with probability $|\alpha|^2$ or $|1\rangle$ with probability $|\beta|^2$.

In Qiskit, a qubit always starts in the $|0\rangle$ state:

```python
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit

qc = QuantumCircuit(1)
qc.draw()
```

## Visualizing State: the Bloch Sphere

Any single-qubit pure state can be drawn as a point on the surface of the **Bloch sphere**. The north pole is $|0\rangle$, the south pole is $|1\rangle$, and the equator holds equal-superposition states such as $|+\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$.
