Every quantum gate you'll ever apply in Qiskit, `h`, `x`, `cx`, all of them, is a matrix. Applying a gate to a state is nothing more than multiplying that matrix by the state's vector. This lesson is about what that multiplication means and how matrices compose.

## Matrix-Vector Multiplication

A $2\times2$ matrix acting on a vector in $\mathbb{C}^2$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \alpha \\ \beta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a\alpha + b\beta \\ c\alpha + d\beta \end{pmatrix}$$

Each output entry is a row of the matrix, dotted against the input vector. This is a **linear map**: scaling the input scales the output by the same factor, and the map distributes over sums, $M(|\psi\rangle + |\phi\rangle) = M|\psi\rangle + M|\phi\rangle$. Every quantum gate has to be linear for exactly this reason: it has to act consistently on a superposition, transforming each amplitude's contribution independently, not "pick one branch and act on that."

## A Concrete Gate: Pauli-X

The Pauli-X matrix is $X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Apply it to $|0\rangle$:

$$X|0\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = |1\rangle$$

That's the bit-flip you already know from Qiskit's `qc.x(0)`, now visible as ordinary matrix arithmetic: $X$ swaps the two rows of whatever vector it's given, which swaps the $|0\rangle$/$|1\rangle$ amplitudes exactly.

## Composing Gates: Matrix Multiplication

Applying gate $A$ and then gate $B$ to a state $|\psi\rangle$ means computing $B(A|\psi\rangle)$, which turns out to equal $(BA)|\psi\rangle$: a single matrix, $BA$, computed by multiplying the two gate matrices together (note the order: $A$ happens first but ends up on the *right*). Matrix multiplication is defined so that this always works, for any sequence of linear maps.

Crucially, $AB \neq BA$ in general, matrix multiplication doesn't commute. This is the formal reason gate order matters in a circuit: `qc.x(0); qc.h(0)` and `qc.h(0); qc.x(0)` are different matrices, and (unless you happen to pick two gates that do commute) different final states.

$$XH = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \\ \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \\ \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad HX = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \\ \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \\ -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} & \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \end{pmatrix}$$

Different matrices ($XH \neq HX$), confirming $H$ then $X$ isn't the same circuit as $X$ then $H$.
